Signs of alcohol poisoning

If someone shows any of these, call emergency services (911 in the US, 999 in the UK, 112 in the EU, 000 in Australia). Don't wait for every sign.

  • Confusion, stupor, or can't be woken up
  • Vomiting, especially while barely conscious
  • Seizures
  • Slow breathing (fewer than 8 breaths a minute) or gaps of 10 seconds or more
  • Pale or bluish skin, low body temperature

Stay with them, keep them on their side so they can't choke, and keep them warm. Coffee, a cold shower or "sleeping it off" won't help. BAC can keep rising after someone passes out, because alcohol is still being absorbed from their stomach.

How the estimate works

1 · Grams of alcohol

Each drink holds volume (mL) × ABV × 0.789 g/mL of pure ethanol. A US standard drink is 14 g, a UK unit is 10 mL (about 7.9 g), and an Australian standard drink is 10 g.

2 · Where it goes: body water

Alcohol spreads through your body water, so BAC is grams ÷ (r × body weight) (Widmark's equation). The factor r is estimated with one of four formulas:

  • Watson: total body water from sex, age, height and weight, with r = TBW ÷ (0.8 × weight) because blood is about 80% water.
  • Seidl: a regression on height and weight from blood measurements.
  • Forrest: based on BMI, so it adjusts for body fat.
  • Widmark: the 1932 constants, 0.68 for men and 0.55 for women.
3 · Absorption and food

Alcohol enters your stomach at an even rate while you drink, then is absorbed with a half-time of about 10 minutes on an empty stomach, 20 with a snack and 40 after a full meal. Food also lets your liver and stomach break down more of it before it reaches your blood, modelled as 10% less (snack) or 20% less (meal) reaching your bloodstream.

4 · Elimination

Your liver clears alcohol at a nearly constant rate, about 0.015 %/h on average and 0.010–0.025 %/h across people. The model uses Michaelis–Menten kinetics (Km = 0.0025 %), which is effectively constant at normal levels and tapers off near zero, as it does in the body. Water, coffee, cold showers and exercise don't speed it up. Only time does.

5 · The shaded range

The band on the chart runs each formula at your clearing rate ±0.003 %/h and shows the lowest and highest result at every minute. Your real curve could still fall outside it.

What it can't account for
  • Medications (some raise BAC or make its effects much stronger), liver disease, pregnancy
  • Genetic differences in alcohol enzymes, carbonation, drinking very fast
  • Mixing alcohol with other drugs, which can be dangerous at any BAC
  • Driving limits change and some apply only to certain drivers. Check your local law.
