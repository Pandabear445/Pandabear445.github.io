Hearth child safety standards

Hearth has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation. These standards apply to the Hearth apps and to kappachat.duckdns.org, the community server run by Hearth's developer (Pandabear445 on GitHub).

If a child is in danger right now, call your local emergency number.

What's banned

Child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) isn't allowed anywhere on Hearth, in any form. That includes:

  • Child sexual abuse material (CSAM): any picture, video, audio or text that shows or describes the sexual abuse of a child, whether it's real, drawn, edited or computer-generated.
  • Grooming: building trust with a child in order to abuse or exploit them, including moving them to private chats for that purpose.
  • Sextortion: threatening a child, or pressuring them, to get sexual images, money or anything else.
  • Sexualising children: sexual comments about minors, and asking minors for sexual content or contact.
  • Trafficking: advertising or arranging the sexual exploitation of a child.
  • Sharing or asking for any of the above, or links to it.

Minimum age

You must be 13 or older to use Hearth, or older if the law where you live says so. Hearth isn't made for children under 13. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 13, we suspend it and delete it.

How to report in the app

  1. For a message: open its menu (the ⋯ button next to it, or a long press) and choose Report message. For a person: open their profile, press ⋯ and choose Report.
  2. Pick Child safety.
  3. Add anything that helps, and press Send report.

Child-safety reports go only to the server's staff, never to the moderators of individual communities. The person you report isn't told who reported them. The app also offers to block them for you.

Messages are end-to-end encrypted, so staff only see the messages you choose to share in the report. Your app shows them to you before you send.

Can't use the app? Email {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} with the username, the server and channel, and when it happened. Don't attach or forward the material itself.

What we do

We treat child-safety reports as the most urgent. When we confirm one, we:

  • Remove the content: messages, profile pictures and other uploads, profile comments, or whole servers.
  • Ban the people involved: we suspend their accounts, and block their networks when needed.
  • Keep what the authorities need: the report, the messages shared in it, and the account details and IP addresses the server has. Removing the content doesn't remove the report.
  • Report it: to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) in the US, and to the local authorities elsewhere.
  • Work with the police when they ask for information through proper legal channels.

Because messages and files are end-to-end encrypted, Hearth can't scan them. We rely on reports from the people who can see them. Profile pictures, banners, server icons and emoji aren't encrypted, so staff can see and remove them directly.

Reporting outside Hearth

You can also report child sexual abuse material straight to NCMEC's CyberTipline, or to a hotline in your country (INHOPE lists them).

Other Hearth servers

Anyone can run a Hearth server. Other servers are run by other people, under their own rules. A report you make in the app goes to the staff of the server you're on. We can't see or moderate servers we don't run. If you see abuse on another server, report it there and to the authorities.
